# Jarvis: what changed

A short list of what this branch changed and what yapd can do now. The commit log has the detail, including every fix that came out of review.

Where it is: work lands on `t3/jarvis-companion-assistant`, is reviewed by GPT-6.1 Sol until nothing you'd run into is left (the narrowest findings go under "Known, left for now"), then goes into `dev`, which the installed yapd (`~/projects/yapd`) runs. The database was backed up before each step went live: `~/.yapd/yapd.before-jarvis.sqlite`, `yapd.before-m1.sqlite`, `yapd.before-m2.sqlite`, `yapd.before-m7.sqlite`, `yapd.before-m8.sqlite`, `yapd.before-followups.sqlite`, `yapd.before-m3.sqlite`, `yapd.before-interrupt.sqlite`, `yapd.before-interrupt-v5.sqlite` and `yapd.before-thread-menu.sqlite`.

Live on `dev` now: everything below but headline first, focus and quiet, which are on `jarvis-m5`, with the menu bar app's side, waiting on review. Also live: the menu bar app's thread menu. Talking over yapd's first seconds stopping it at once, muting threads, rig's threads, and agents' questions read out and answered by voice are waiting on their first checks with you.

## Talk to it (the shortcut)

- The shortcut goes to one brain that knows your T3 Code threads: what each is doing, what it's called aloud, what you started it with and what yapd last said about it. Ask "what's going on?", "what's the Tezos one doing right now?", "who needs me?", "how much Claude have I got left?", "what did I miss?", "say that again", plain questions, or dictate new work as before.
- It picks the thread you mean by how it sounds and what it's about, so misheard names like "MiNAS SV2" or "my Tesla's migration" land first time. Nothing in yapd overrules its pick by matching words, which is what sank the reverted attempt.
- At most one question at a time, always naming what it's choosing between, never in the same words twice. Whatever you say next answers it or replaces it. Left unanswered, it's asked once more a minute later in other words, then let go with a word (your rule).
- "It" means what you were just listening to. Work it started in the last half hour isn't started twice.
- Tell a thread something ("tell the Tezos one to use the Mina fee table"): a busy one gets it in the turn under way, or in T3 Code's own queue when you say "when it's done" or the turn is waiting on you, and yapd says which. It names the thread when it isn't the one you were just hearing about, and asks first only when it isn't sure which.
- "Stop the Tezos one" or "stop working", then "carry on" if you change your mind; "scratch that" withdraws a message still in the queue. "Stop and do this instead" stops its turn, then tells it once it shows stopped; still busy fifteen seconds on, it isn't told, and yapd says so. If T3 Code holds it in the queue the stop held, yapd says so, and "carry on" lets it go. A bare "stop" still only stops yapd talking.
- "No, the Mina one" to its question does that instead, and "yes, but once it's done" or "yes, and then tell it…" does what you add. Two things in one breath ("stop the Tezos one and tell the Mina one…") are done in order, the first said at once; if one doesn't go, it says what it left.
- Nothing is ever sent twice behind your back: the same words to a thread that hasn't answered since get "I sent that a minute ago, sir. Again?", and a message yapd can't confirm got there, even across a restart, gets "Send it again?", to which saying the same words again is a yes.
- Answers come before any update waiting to be read.
- Heard to the end, an answer like a thread's status gets the same few seconds for a reply as an update: "tell it to fix the tests", "stop it" or "show me that" act on the thread it was about, and "thanks" or "skip" end it without the model.
- "Stop", "skip" or "enough" over an update skips it. "What did I miss?" leaves out what's about to be read anyway, and says how many are coming up.
- `POST /utterances` takes a typed request the same way, for scripts and other apps.

## Needs you (live, waiting on its live check with you)

- An agent's question, on this Mac or rig, is read out as it comes, in its own words, a part at a time, with the option it recommends said as yapd's pick ("I'd go with Blue."). Answer with an option, a few, "yes" to the pick, or your own words, which it gets as you said them, sent back only to the machine that asked; "what are the options?", "later" and "what's the question?" work too. Only an option's exact name skips the model: as the agent wrote it, in any case, with a full stop after it at most ("C++.", "No tests."), or, where you can pick several, exact names joined by "and" or commas. Anything else goes to the model, which is told yapd's pick and whether you heard the question to the end: cut short, what doesn't name an option ("the last one", "yeah, that works") asks it again in full. The model handles a plain "yes", "your pick", "the second one", "four" for "4 workers", "C" for "C++", "No, tests", part of a name or "all of them". What it makes of them goes as your own words unless it's an option's exact name. A message you dictate to a thread waiting on a question you've heard goes as its answer; one waiting on something you haven't heard yet gets your message in its queue, since steering it in would cancel what it waits on, and you're told it'll go once that's dealt with; with parts after it, those are asked first, and if you let them go, leave them unanswered or put them off too often, your message still goes, with them left out. Approvals are no longer announced, since your agents run with full access: "who needs me?" still lists them, and you can approve one by voice, only with "approve", "approve it", "allow" or "allow it" as your whole answer ("yes, approve it" and "approve it, thanks" too). A plain "yes", "sure", "OK", "go ahead" or "do it" never approves one, however harmless it looks, nor does an approve with anything more to it ("approve it unless the tests fail", "approve the Mina one", "go ahead, I'll approve the other one later"), which gets "It needs an 'approve', so I've left it waiting for you in T3 Code." An approve with more after it ("approve it, and tell the Mina one to wait") is asked again on its own, with the rest said to be left. Nothing you said before you heard a question answers it, and a quick "yes" or an option's name said back to an answer is never taken as one: an agent's question that comes up while you can still follow up an answer is asked once yapd has dealt with what you said. Failures, usage limits and finished work from agents without hooks are said once, and a finished turn from its hook or from T3 Code, never both. After a restart, what still waits on you is asked once each machine's T3 Code has caught up, so rig being down holds up nothing here. A question whose machine drops out is put by and asked again once that machine is back, even across a restart; an answer you give meanwhile isn't sent, and yapd says so: "I couldn't get your answer to it, sir: I can't reach rig right now. I'll ask you again once I can." Secrets are never taken by voice, nor kept when you say one.
- Updates are tied to their T3 Code thread, so "tell it to…" after one goes into that thread.

## Mutes (live, waiting on its live check with you)

- "Mute it" over or after a noisy thread's update, or "mute the Tezos one", keeps its progress and finished work to yourself, for good or "for an hour"; its questions, failures, limits and the answers to what you sent it still come through, and "what did I miss?" sums it up in a line. "Unmute it", "what's muted?", "unmute everything" and "scratch that" work too, and rig's threads are muted by their thread there. A muted terminal session is unmuted by what "what's muted?" calls it. Over an update, only "mute it", "unmute it" and the like, and "scratch that" right after, are taken there; anything else about mutes, like "mute the Tezos one", gets "Ask me that with the shortcut." Nothing about it reaches an agent.
- The API's state lists the threads with the latest updates, each with its project's icon and whether it's muted, and threads can be muted, heard again and their icons fetched by key.
- The menu bar lists those threads with their project icons, how long ago each finished and whether it's muted: clicking one says its latest update again, and the Mute menu, or Option over a row on macOS 15 and later, mutes it or takes the mute off. An older yapd still gets the old Hear Again list.
- A terminal session's or an archived thread's older update can't be heard again from the menu: its row is greyed out, and can still be muted.

## Headline first, focus and quiet (on `jarvis-m5`)

- Updates are said as a headline of about fifteen words, and "more" over it or just after says the rest at once, rendered as the headline plays; over yapd's first seconds the model decides. What needs you is said in full, question and all. Real headlines from six test messages kept the question and the outcome, at 8 to 15 words.
- Progress, like "pass 7 is running" or "still waiting on CI", is held for a digest, told from news by what yapd last said about the same work. The digest comes once it's been quiet a minute, with three held or one held twenty minutes, at most every twenty minutes, never in focus or quiet; you can reply to it or cut it off, and only what you heard to the end counts as heard. Progress of work you've since heard the outcome of, or of a thread you mute since, never comes up in it, nor in the rundown.
- "Focus", "focus for two hours", "goodnight", "quiet for an hour", "I'm back", "talk shorter" and "give me everything", by the shortcut or over an update, in so many words; anything else about them goes to the model, or over an update gets "Ask me that with the shortcut." Over something that asks you a question, only "focus", "focus or quiet for…", "goodnight", "I'm back", "back to normal" and "talk shorter" are taken that way: "keep it short", "tell me everything", "give me everything", "normal mode" or "full updates" may be your answer, so they go to the model, which can send them to the agent. Said partway through an update that needs you, a mode change or a mute is done and said, then the update carries on from where it stopped, and counts as heard only once it's said to the end. A bare "quiet" still only stops yapd. Focus and quiet keep your mute rule: what needs you and the answer to your own message still come through. "I'm back" gives the rundown straight away; a mode that ends on its own, or from the API, offers it once.
- What finished while yapd was down is held as "… finished while I was offline", with no model call, each once, ever. The answer to a message you sent it, by voice or the shortcut, is summed up and said instead, muted or not; a run you stopped, or one so short you were likely watching it, isn't brought up; a failure from then is said.
- The updates you heard last can be heard again after a restart, under the same ids, and a reply to one goes to its thread through T3 Code.
- The API's state has `mode`, `until` and `waiting`, and `PUT /state` changes only what it names.
- The menu bar app has a Mode menu (Normal, Focus for an Hour, Quiet until Morning) and says when focus or quiet ends; its icon gets a moon in focus or quiet and a dot while a thread waits on you, and "N Threads Waiting on You" has yapd say who, as "who needs me?" would. With a yapd from before modes it shows none of this.

## Show me

- "Show me what's running", "show me that PR", "show me my usage" or "what I missed" puts a card in a panel under the menu bar icon as yapd says it; "hide that" takes it down, and "open that PR" opens it in your browser, https only. A thread waiting on a command that can't be read aloud gets its card with the answer, and "say that again" puts it back up. "Show me the question" puts an agent's question up as it's asked again.
- The API gains `/cards`, `/threads` and `/journal` to read from.

## Rig

- Rig's threads count as much as this Mac's: the desk, "who needs me?", "what's running?", cards, agents' questions and messages or stops by voice cover both, each sent only to its own machine, through an SSH tunnel to rig's T3 Code whose token is fetched at startup and kept in memory only. While rig can't be reached, yapd says why the first time something needs rig, then only that it still can't see rig's threads, and this Mac's carry on regardless. Needs rig on a yapd with `yapd t3`.

## Faster

- "Yes" or "go ahead" to an update that asked something, and "thanks" or "skip it", are handled without the model: about two seconds saved on the most common replies.
- Short lines like "Right away, sir." are rendered once and kept; your usual lines are written in your `YAPD_STYLE` once and rendered at startup.
- Your own lines for "on it", in `YAPD_ON_IT` separated by `|`, take turns in place of the written one when a reply is passed on, a message or an answer in your own words goes by voice or new work starts, never the same twice in a row.
- No more ~2 s wait for a fresh Codex thread after a quiet spell (one update in five); three are kept ready.
- The speaker gets ready while an update renders: about a second saved on nearly every update.
- You can interrupt from the first word of an update, an answer or one of its questions: it turns down at your first sound and stops once you're clearly talking, which over its first three seconds takes about a fifth of a second, and the model decides from what you said, told it may begin with yapd's own words; if only its own voice stopped it, it picks up again a word or two back, without a word. When yapd is cut off, the model is also given the rest of the line it hadn't said yet and decides from that: a bare "okay" or "thanks" said before the agent's question carries on, so you hear it, and doesn't count as talk not meant for yapd; said once you've heard the question, it's taken as before, "okay" as a yes and "thanks" as enough. In real calls an okay before the question carried on 2 of 2 times; after it, "Okay." sent a yes 2 of 2 times and "Thanks." ended it 2 of 2 times with the final wording, where an earlier one sent it as a yes; an okay over the question's last word sent a yes, as did "Sounds good."; and "Okay, thanks." to a failure ended it.
- "Who needs me?" and usage need no model call; a status question takes one, against a minute or more in the reverted attempt.

## Under the hood

- A versioned SQLite store in `~/.yapd/yapd.sqlite`, with a journal of everything heard, said and done. It survives restarts and feeds "what did I miss".
- A live link to T3 Code's threads that reconnects and catches up on its own, and actions on threads (message, steer, stop, approve, answer, archive, rename, snooze, search, usage). Messages, stops and starts by voice go through them now.
- Every message, stop and start is written down before it goes out, under ids that make T3 Code do it once however often it's sent. A restart only looks; it never sends. Turned off and on, nothing more goes out for what you said before.
- `scripts/m2-probe.ts` checks those T3 Code receipts once, on a thread it starts for itself; it sends nothing without `--send`, and `--thread` repeats only the restart check, on a thread it started.
- Groundwork, not switched on yet: streaming plus first-sentence playback for faster speech.
- `scripts/brain-eval.ts` replays real phrases against the live model to check it picks the right thread.
- yapd's local API turns away anything a web page sends, so no site you visit can have it start work or talk to your threads.

## Known, left for now

- A held update from a terminal session can't be heard again from the menu.
- A digest played to an empty room counts as heard.
- An update heard before a restart from rig is answered the way rig's hooks are, which may not reach it.
- "I'm back" said over an update that doesn't need you ends it there, and the rundown comes right after as an answer.
- A mode change said over an update is kept in the journal as a reply whose intent is "mute".
- Progress held before a thread failed can still come up in the digest after you heard the failure: only an update said about the same work makes it out of date.
- "Focus is over, sir. Want the rundown?" waiting behind another line as you start focus again is still said.

- With exactly two lines of your own for going ahead, one picked for something that never plays can let the other come twice in a row.
- After a message was moved from the queue into the turn under way, "I sent that a minute ago. Again?" can be asked for its same words until ten minutes have passed.
- With an older yapd on rig, a connection dropped while starting work there is said as "I can't reach rig" rather than "it may have started".
- "Show Last Card" just after closing a card can come to nothing if the close reaches yapd after it: choose it again.
- A card closed while the app has lost touch with yapd is taken down when it's back, even if something else showed it again meanwhile.
- Rig's hooks are tied to their thread only for what's shown and muted, so "tell it to…" after one of rig's updates goes the way hooks always have.
- A mute on a session that isn't a T3 Code thread, like one in a terminal, stays on that session only, and is listed by "what's muted?" until you unmute it.
- A thread muted by its session, before yapd found the session was the thread's, shows muted in the menu and is unmuted with it, but the brain doesn't see it as muted: "unmute the Tezos one" still works, while "what's muted?" names the session.
- The answer to a message you sent a muted thread is said when yapd can tell which turn took it: by the turn's prompt through the hooks, or, when T3 Code tells of the turn first or the agent has no hooks, by when T3 Code says it began. After a restart in the middle of a turn with hooks, it's kept quiet like the rest.
- On a muted thread, a turn that begins within a minute of a message you sent its last turn may be read out, as if it answered you.
- Heard again from the menu, a thread's update that was first heard under its session, before yapd tied it to the thread, is muted and unmuted by the thread's row while it plays, but by its session once it has finished, so "unmute it" then finds nothing to unmute.
- A question you put off with "later", or that made way for another, isn't brought back if yapd restarts before it comes round again. It still waits in T3 Code, and "who needs me?" lists it.
- "Thanks" or "skip" said back to an answer, while yapd's own "which one?" for the next step of what you asked waits behind it, lets that step go; yapd says so.
- Showing or hiding a card while an agent's question waits on you asks it again straight away, and the third time in a row lets it go: ask for it when you're ready.
- For Yes and No options you hear "No it is, sir.", which can sound as if yapd is turning you down.
- If T3 Code is slow to hand over a question as it arrives, you're only told one is waiting; "what's the question?" reads it to you.
- An agent's question with an option described by nothing never shows up: T3 Code fails the turn for it and the thread can't start another until T3 Code restarts it. yapd only says the thread failed.
- A part of a question you skip is left out of the answer, and Claude isn't told it was skipped. For Claude, a question let go and one answered with nothing read the same.
- Codex asks questions only in Plan mode. T3 Code's own "resume session" question, when it brings back an old Claude session, is read out like an agent's.
- Catching up, if yapd has to ask "which one?" about your follow-up, the next "what did I miss?" may say "Nothing else." though an update is still unheard.
- After twenty follow-ups in a row, "what did I miss?" can reach back to an update from earlier that day you'd moved on from.
- Over yapd's first three seconds, its own voice can stop it as if you'd spoken, before the echo cancellation has learnt it, and it picks up again a word or two back, which this early is the line's start. In a simulation with Silero, Whisper and these rules, with its voice dying away as the echo cancellation learns it, 22 of 48 lines on a new voice processor stopped, 4 of them twice, adding a median of about 3.6 s per stopped line: about 1.2 s each time it was picked up again without the model, about 3.7 s each time what Whisper heard went to the model. That was with yapd's own words still picked up without the model; they now go to it too, so each of those costs about a model call more. Held at one level for all three seconds, the worst it gets, it's worse: 38 of 48 stopped, most 4 times, adding a median 8 s. Your 2026-09-29 logs cut 9 of 13 such lines. To count it on your speakers, `grep -c "Stopped for its own voice over its first seconds" ~/Library/Logs/yapd.log` gives each time it picked up again after its own voice, or what the model took for it, which can include an "okay" said before the agent's question; your talk that wasn't meant for it is logged apart, as "Picked up again over its first seconds after talk not meant for it". Playing quieter after a stop didn't help in the simulation.
- Over those seconds, a few short things are dealt with without the model. Over an update, all Whisper hears goes to the Responder, but a bare "stop", "skip" or "enough", or a thanks or an okay when nothing waits on you, ends it there; when all of it may have been yapd's own voice, the update is left for "what did I miss?". Over an answer or a question, a short "thank you", "bye" or "Thanks for watching!", only "uh" or "sir", or the same words three times, is picked up again a word or two back, and what never needs the model, like "enough" or "say that again", is done at once. Silence, when Whisper hears nothing at all or fails, is picked up again too, in about 1 s. Everything else goes to the model, however short, told it may be yapd's own voice: "4.", "C.", "Stop, thanks.", "Okay, stop." over its "stopped", "That's it.", and yapd's own words too, like "The agent stopped." over its "the agent stopped". The model picks up again when it takes it for yapd's voice, at about the cost of a model call, about 2.6 s, rather than about 1 s. What you add after a pause is given to it as surely yours, even in yapd's words, like "Yapd." after its last word "site" in "yapd or the docs site?", and is never picked up again as nothing because of what came before it, like its own "Yapd." ahead of your "Yapd, yapd.". So is your answer to which project new work goes in, when it's written up.
- Over those seconds, yapd's own voice heard as other words, like "is important." for its "importer", goes to the model, which is told it may be yapd's: in 9 real calls on such words it picked up again or asked again every time, but it can still take one for yours. Heard as a thanks or an okay, it ends the update without the model, and the model would end it too, but the update is left for "what did I miss?". Over an answer or one of its questions it can go the same way; an answer cut short is still yours to catch up on. On the other side, your "Cool." heard as "Coo-yo." was once taken for yapd's own "Claude on", and it carried on.
- If your speakers or room keep yapd's voice going more than about 0.3 s after it stops, like Bluetooth, its own voice can go past about 0.45 s after it stops more often; the model is still told it may be yapd's, but picking up again then is logged as talk not meant for it.
