# Jarvis: what changed

A short list of what this branch changed and what yapd can do now. The commit log has the detail, including every fix that came out of review.

Where it is: work lands on `t3/jarvis-companion-assistant`, is reviewed by GPT-6.1 Sol until nothing you'd run into is left (the narrowest findings go under "Known, left for now"), then goes into `dev`, which the installed yapd (`~/projects/yapd`) runs. The database was backed up before each step went live: `~/.yapd/yapd.before-jarvis.sqlite`, `yapd.before-m1.sqlite`, `yapd.before-m2.sqlite`, `yapd.before-m7.sqlite`, `yapd.before-m8.sqlite`, `yapd.before-followups.sqlite`, `yapd.before-m3.sqlite`, `yapd.before-interrupt.sqlite`, `yapd.before-interrupt-v5.sqlite`, `yapd.before-thread-menu.sqlite`, `yapd.before-m5-off.sqlite`, `yapd.before-thread-panel.sqlite`, `yapd.before-question-heard.sqlite`, `yapd.before-m6a.sqlite`, `yapd.before-m6a-fix.sqlite`, `yapd.before-m6b.sqlite`, `yapd.before-mute.sqlite`, `yapd.before-m6c.sqlite`, `yapd.before-catchup-clear.sqlite`, `yapd.before-subagents.sqlite`, `yapd.before-app-b.sqlite`, `yapd.before-m4b1.sqlite`, `yapd.before-m6d.sqlite`, `yapd.before-m4b2.sqlite` and `yapd.before-reminders-menu.sqlite`.

Live on `dev` now: everything below, headline first, focus and quiet, off keeping everything with catching up, the thread panels beside the menu's recent threads, research threads read back as answers, reminders, offers and usage warnings, the emergency "Mute.", memories and routines, clearing the catch-up by voice, a thread's subagents kept to yapd unless you ask for them, tidying threads by voice, replying after yapd's lines, and the menu's Reminders included, with the menu bar app installed. ## Talk to it (the shortcut)

- The shortcut goes to one brain that knows your T3 Code threads: what each is doing, what it's called aloud, what you started it with and what yapd last said about it. Ask "what's going on?", "what's the Tezos one doing right now?", "who needs me?", "how much Claude have I got left?", "catch me up", "say that again", plain questions, or dictate new work as before.
- It picks the thread you mean by how it sounds and what it's about, so misheard names like "MiNAS SV2" or "my Tesla's migration" land first time. Nothing in yapd overrules its pick by matching words, which is what sank the reverted attempt.
- At most one question at a time, always naming what it's choosing between, never in the same words twice. Whatever you say next answers it or replaces it. Left unanswered, it's asked once more a minute later in other words, then let go with a word (your rule).
- "It" means what you were just listening to. Work it started in the last half hour isn't started twice.
- Research threads (not checked live yet): "find out whether Apple shipped an API for third-party glucose sensors on the Watch" or "look into why rig's tunnel drops overnight" starts a thread that finds out and tells you, in the project it's about, or, about none of them, in T3 Code's scratch project ("Right away, sir. Looking into it, on Sol."), its agent told to change nothing on your machine. Nothing about code, a build, tests, a branch or a pull request goes there: with no project named, it asks which, and one that could go either way gets "For the glucose question, is that for yapd, or shall I just look into it?" "Look into it in scratch" puts it there too, except anything about code. The writer decides; yapd only checks that the scratch project it picked is T3 Code's own, by its folder. Rig gets it once rig's yapd is updated. The old read-through of a project before writing the prompt ("Looking through yapd first.") is gone.
- The first turn of any work yapd starts is read as the answer to what you asked, headline first, then "more": however quick or slight, whatever you muted, in focus too, and across a restart, even when it ended while yapd was restarting; it's never introduced as "No project". In quiet it waits for the morning's rundown, even when quiet begins while it waits to be said or a dictation cuts it off, and while yapd is off for your catch-up. A turn of work yapd sent, its hook skipping as quick, or dropping as slight when it couldn't tell by id whether it was the first, as when it couldn't find the thread in time, is said from T3 Code's word instead, which also covers quick answers to messages you sent by voice. A slight reply to a message yapd sent, its hook tied to its thread, stays skipped.
- Tell a thread something ("tell the Tezos one to use the Mina fee table"): a busy one gets it in the turn under way, or in T3 Code's own queue when you say "when it's done" or the turn is waiting on you, and yapd says which. It names the thread when it isn't the one you were just hearing about, and asks first only when it isn't sure which.
- "Stop the Tezos one" or "stop working", then "carry on" if you change your mind; "scratch that" or "undo that" takes back the newest thing you changed in the last two minutes: a message still in the queue, a mute, a reminder you just set or cancelled, something you just had yapd remember or forget, or threads you just had it archive, rename, snooze, settle or pin, whichever came last, whatever thread you were just hearing about, so a message sent a minute before a reminder is never withdrawn in its place; "withdraw that message" or "take back the message to the Mina one" takes back the message, never a reminder you set since, and "cancel that reminder" only the reminder, whatever was sent after it, even one for a time set while you were hearing about a thread. "Stop and do this instead" stops its turn, then tells it once it shows stopped; still busy fifteen seconds on, it isn't told, and yapd says so. If T3 Code holds it in the queue the stop held, yapd says so, and "carry on" lets it go. A bare "stop" still only stops yapd talking.
- "No, the Mina one" to its question does that instead, and "yes, but once it's done" or "yes, and then tell it…" does what you add. Two things in one breath ("stop the Tezos one and tell the Mina one…") are done in order, the first said at once; if one doesn't go, it says what it left.
- Nothing is ever sent twice behind your back: the same words to a thread that hasn't answered since get "I sent that a minute ago, sir. Again?", and a message yapd can't confirm got there, even across a restart, gets "Send it again?", to which saying the same words again is a yes.
- Answers come before any update waiting to be read.
- Heard to the end, an answer like a thread's status gets six seconds for a reply, twice an update's three (not on `dev` yet; it was three): "tell it to fix the tests", "stop it" or "show me that" act on the thread it was about, and "thanks" or "skip" end it without the model.
- "Stop", "skip" or "enough" over an update skips it. A catch-up leaves out what's about to be read anyway, and says how many are coming up.
- "Mute." is the emergency off (not live yet): on its own, it turns yapd off at once, as the menu's Turn Off does, wherever yapd hears you: over or right after an update, an answer, a catch-up, a replay or the digest, over its own question or an agent's, its first three seconds included, over or right after a notice, like a failure, and over or right after the rest of a line it plays through after talk not meant for it, say from a TV, where nothing else you say stops it, even after someone else's talk that stopped yapd and is still being worked out, or with talk right after it, like your phone answered, and by the shortcut, even while it's still working out what you asked before, which that stops. No model, nothing said back. A notice never turns down for talk over it, yours, a TV's or a video's, so none of that can keep it low and then have it marked heard, and nor does the rest of a line played through after talk not meant for yapd. Over either, only what's short enough to be "Mute.", even said again and again, about two and a half seconds of speech at most, is transcribed: a TV costs no transcription, and nothing waits on it. Nothing is transcribed there while Whisper is still loading, and what Whisper takes more than four seconds over is taken for something else, so neither holds what's next up long. The few seconds of listening after either are kept when nothing waits to be said; when something does, like the answer after "Let me check", they're half a second, which takes the place of the pause there always was between lines, so what's next starts about two tenths of a second later than it did and hears "Mute." itself. A "Mute." you'd begun in that half second or just before it, over its last words, or begin a tenth of a second after, is heard out first, and so is one you begin within about a second of someone else's talk stopping; talk begun earlier and still going, like a TV's, holds nothing up, and what's next is never held up more than four seconds past that half second. Everything waiting stays unheard for your catch-up, nothing reaches any thread, and an agent's question it was reading stays waiting in T3 Code, as from the menu. "Mute, mute, mute" counts too; "Mute?", asked, isn't the off switch: by the shortcut the model makes of it what it will, and over yapd it's what it always was, a stop at most. "Mute it", "mute that for an hour", "mute the Tezos one" and "what's muted?" mean what they did. "Mute." acts the moment it's heard on its own, never waiting to see whether you carry on, so "Mute." then a pause of more than about 0.7 s before "the Tezos one" turns yapd off: say "mute the Tezos one" in one breath. Over yapd's first seconds of a line about muting, it may be yapd's own voice, so there it only stops yapd, and a notice plays on. The log says "Turned off: he said mute".
- Back on by voice (not live yet): while yapd is off, press the shortcut and say "unmute", "turn on", "turn it on", "turn yourself on" or "turn back on", and it comes back as the menu's Turn On brings it back, offering what you missed, except that what you dictate right after, even pressed for before it's back on, is kept and answered once it's back on, where the menu's Turn On drops a dictation under way. Unlike "Mute.", it waits its turn behind anything you'd asked before it. "Unmute?" or "Turn on?", asked, is the model's to answer. While yapd is on, a bare "unmute" means what it did; anything else said while it's off is answered as before.
- `POST /utterances` takes a typed request the same way, for scripts and other apps.

## Needs you (live, waiting on its live check with you)

- An agent's question, on this Mac or rig, is read out as it comes, in its own words, a part at a time, with the option it recommends said as yapd's pick ("I'd go with Blue."). Answer with an option, a few, "yes" to the pick, or your own words, which it gets as you said them, sent back only to the machine that asked; "what are the options?", "later" and "what's the question?" work too. Only an option's exact name skips the model: as the agent wrote it, in any case, with a full stop after it at most ("C++.", "No tests."), or, where you can pick several, exact names joined by "and" or commas. Anything else goes to the model, which is told yapd's pick and whether you heard the question to the end. Talk over it and the model is also told what yapd hadn't said yet, and judges what you'd heard: a plain "yes" or "no" to a yes-or-no question is the option that means it, even before the question's end when the rest only adds detail ("…now, knowing you may need to reposition its icon?"). Once you've begun hearing yapd's pick, a plain "yes" is to the pick, and only the pick goes without you naming it: anything else is asked again in full. When the rest could change what a yes agrees to, or your words may be about options you hadn't heard yet ("the last one", "yeah, that works"), it's asked again in full. Over its own voice in its first seconds, or cut short by a dictation, where how far it got isn't known, what doesn't name an option asks it again in full. The model handles a plain "yes", "your pick", "the second one", "four" for "4 workers", "C" for "C++", "No, tests", part of a name or "all of them". What it makes of them goes as your own words unless it's an option's exact name. A message you dictate to a thread waiting on a question you've heard goes as its answer; one waiting on something you haven't heard yet gets your message in its queue, since steering it in would cancel what it waits on, and you're told it'll go once that's dealt with; with parts after it, those are asked first, and if you let them go, leave them unanswered or put them off too often, your message still goes, with them left out. Approvals are no longer announced, since your agents run with full access: "who needs me?" still lists them, and you can approve one by voice, only with "approve", "approve it", "allow" or "allow it" as your whole answer ("yes, approve it" and "approve it, thanks" too). A plain "yes", "sure", "OK", "go ahead" or "do it" never approves one, however harmless it looks, nor does an approve with anything more to it ("approve it unless the tests fail", "approve the Mina one", "go ahead, I'll approve the other one later"), which gets "It needs an 'approve', so I've left it waiting for you in T3 Code." An approve with more after it ("approve it, and tell the Mina one to wait") is asked again on its own, with the rest said to be left. Nothing you said before you heard a question answers it, and a quick "yes" or an option's name said back to an answer is never taken as one: an agent's question that comes up while you can still follow up an answer is asked once yapd has dealt with what you said. Failures, usage limits and finished work from agents without hooks are said once, and a finished turn from its hook or from T3 Code, never both. After a restart, what still waits on you is asked once each machine's T3 Code has caught up, so rig being down holds up nothing here. A question whose machine drops out is put by and asked again once that machine is back, even across a restart; an answer you give meanwhile isn't sent, and yapd says so: "I couldn't get your answer to it, sir: I can't reach rig right now. I'll ask you again once I can." An agent asking for a secret gets it only in T3 Code, never by voice: a message to its thread is held back while it waits on one, or, when it looks like a secret, on a question you'd type an answer to.
- Updates are tied to their T3 Code thread, so "tell it to…" after one goes into that thread.

## Mutes (live, waiting on its live check with you)

- "Mute it" over or after a noisy thread's update, or "mute the Tezos one", keeps its progress and finished work to yourself, for good or "for an hour"; its questions, failures, limits and the answers to what you sent it still come through, and asking what it said catches you up on it. "Unmute it", "what's muted?", "unmute everything" and "scratch that" work too, and rig's threads are muted by their thread there. A muted terminal session is unmuted by what "what's muted?" calls it. A bare "Mute." mutes no thread: it turns yapd off. Over an update, only "mute it", "unmute it" and the like, and "scratch that" right after, are taken there; anything else about mutes, like "mute the Tezos one", gets "Ask me that with the shortcut." Nothing about it reaches an agent.
- The API's state lists the threads with the latest updates, each with its project's icon and whether it's muted, and threads can be muted, heard again and their icons fetched by key.
- The menu bar lists those threads with their project icons, how long ago each finished and whether it's muted: clicking one says its latest update again, and hovering one opens its panel beside the menu, as CodexBar's do. The panel shows what the thread is doing and for how long, its branch, its pull request and its checks, whether it's muted and until when, the latest update in full and what came before it, heard or not; under it are Hear Again, Mute, Mute for an Hour or Unmute, and Open Pull Request. It stays current while it's open. An older yapd still gets the old Hear Again list.
- A terminal session's or an archived thread's older update can't be heard again from the menu: its row is greyed out, and can still be muted.

## Headline first, focus and quiet (live, waiting on its live check with you)

- Updates are said as a headline of about fifteen words, and "more" over it or just after says the rest at once, rendered as the headline plays; over yapd's first seconds the model decides. What needs you is said in full, question and all. Real headlines from six test messages kept the question and the outcome, at 8 to 15 words.
- Progress, like "pass 7 is running" or "still waiting on CI", is held for a digest, told from news by what yapd last said about the same work. The digest comes once it's been quiet a minute, with three held or one held twenty minutes, at most every twenty minutes, never in focus or quiet; you can reply to it or cut it off, and only what you heard to the end counts as heard. Progress of work you've since heard the outcome of, or of a thread you mute since, never comes up in it, nor in the rundown.
- "Focus", "focus for two hours", "goodnight", "quiet for an hour", "I'm back", "talk shorter" and "give me everything", by the shortcut or over an update, in so many words; anything else about them goes to the model, or over an update gets "Ask me that with the shortcut." Over something that asks you a question, only "focus", "focus or quiet for…", "goodnight", "I'm back", "back to normal" and "talk shorter" are taken that way: "keep it short", "tell me everything", "give me everything", "normal mode" or "full updates" may be your answer, so they go to the model, which can send them to the agent. Said partway through an update that needs you, a mode change or a mute is done and said, then the update carries on from where it stopped, and counts as heard only once it's said to the end. A bare "quiet" still only stops yapd. Focus and quiet keep your mute rule: what needs you and the answer to your own message still come through. "I'm back" gives the rundown straight away; a mode that ends on its own, or from the API, offers it once.
- The updates you heard last can be heard again after a restart, under the same ids, and a reply to one goes to its thread through T3 Code.
- The API's state has `mode`, `until` and `waiting`, and `PUT /state` changes only what it names.
- The menu bar app has a Mode menu (Normal, Focus for an Hour, Quiet until Morning) and says when focus or quiet ends; its icon gets a moon in focus or quiet and a dot while a thread waits on you, and "N Threads Waiting on You" has yapd say who, as "who needs me?" would. With a yapd from before modes it shows none of this.

## Off keeps everything, and catching up (live, waiting on its live check with you)

- Off no longer drops anything: what finishes while yapd is off is summed up and kept, unheard, never said on its own, not even once it's back on. That's every update, what needs you and the answer to your own message too, a run that fails or hits a limit, and a turn with no hook; a muted thread's stays muted. Turning off keeps what was waiting to be said, what was being summed up and what it was reading, unheard, and lets agents' hooks go at once, so none waits on yapd.
- The shortcut still works while it's off: what you ask is answered, then it's quiet again, with no listening for a reply. A question it asks you then, like "which one?", waits for your next press, and isn't asked again of its own accord.
- Turned back on with anything kept meanwhile, it says once "You missed five, sir. Want the rundown?" first: a yes gives you the catch-up, anything else lets it go with nothing marked heard. Agents' questions still waiting are asked as before, after it. Left unanswered, or with a press over it that comes to nothing, the offer is let go without a word and never asked again, and once you've been caught up some other way, it isn't made at all. What was waiting or being summed up as you turned it off counts in the number, and so does what you still haven't heard from an earlier time off that week; with nothing new since it was last on, it isn't offered. Starting with yapd on, or turning it on again while it's on, stops nothing you're asking.
- "Catch me up", "what did I miss on Distribox?", "anything from rig since lunch?", "what did the muted ones say?": no phrase is built in, the model decides it's a catch-up and of what, a thread, a project, a machine or since when. yapd first reads you failures, limits and questions in an agent's words as they were kept, question and options and all, then the answer to what you asked a thread and any turn it couldn't sum up, four at most, then briefs you on the rest in about ninety words, a thread at a time, eight at most, then how much is left, which "more" gets you; with nothing left, "more" gives the detail of what it told you. Nothing new gets "You're all caught up, sir." or "Nothing new from Distribox, sir." without the model. A project can be named by a word of its name, like "connectors"; one named nowhere gets "I couldn't tell which one you meant, sir." "Catch the Tezos one up on…" is a message to that thread, not a catch-up. What it told you counts as heard only once you've heard it to the end. What came in while yapd was off, whenever that week, and what needs you stay in it for the week, however often you turn yapd off and on since. A catch-up of everything leaves muted threads out, and unheard; ask about one by name, a terminal session too. What's being read to you as you ask isn't told again, nor counted in the menu while it's read. A failure whose turn came in while yapd was off leads the catch-up on its own, even once you mute its thread. It only reads: "tell the Distribox one to…" after it is a request like any other.
- Heard means heard: an update read to the end, a catch-up, a digest or the menu telling of it to the end, a newer line about the same work heard to the end, or your "stop" or "skip". Progress is now out of date only once you hear the outcome, not as it's queued. The digest and the rundown a mode offers as it ends leave out what came in while yapd was off; "I'm back" gives the whole catch-up.
- An unanswered rundown offer no longer holds a thread's question back a minute: the question is asked at once and the offer comes back after. The answer to what you said back through a hook before a restart is said, never held as progress or kept quiet. Muting a thread later never keeps quiet what needs you, or answers you, that came in while yapd was off.
- Clearing the catch-up: "clear the catch-up", "skip the catch-up", "mark everything as heard" or "I don't need the catch-up", no phrase built in, sets aside everything a catch-up would tell you now, exactly what "Catch Me Up (N)" counts, without reading any of it: "Cleared, sir: fifty-three updates set aside.", or "There's nothing to catch up on, sir." The count drops to nothing at once. Agents' questions still waiting are untouched, still asked and still in "who needs me?", and so are muted threads and what's being read to you. It's all of it or nothing ("I can only clear the whole catch-up, sir."), needs your own word, so nothing faint over yapd's voice clears it, and works while yapd is off. "Scratch that" within two minutes brings back exactly what it set aside that you haven't heard since: "Brought them back, sir." `POST /catchup/clear` does the same for the menu, answering `{"cleared": n}`, and "scratch that" by voice takes that back too. The menu's Clear Catch-Up, right under "Catch Me Up (N)" and only while there's anything, calls it at once, with no asking first, since "scratch that" brings it back; the count drops as the state says so, and it works while yapd is off.
- The API's state has `unheard`, and each of its `recent` rows its own; `/journal` says why an update wasn't read out (`unsaid`); `POST /utterances` works while off.
- The menu bar app says how many updates of a thread you haven't heard beside it, unless it's muted, and offers "Catch Me Up (N)" while there's anything: choose it and yapd catches you up aloud, as "catch me up" by the shortcut would, even while it's off. "N Threads Waiting on You" works while it's off too. Clicking a thread still says its latest update again, while yapd is on, including one kept while it was off. With a yapd from before it, the menu shows none of this, and asks nothing while yapd is off.

## Reminders, offers and usage warnings (not checked live yet)

- "Remind me at three to call the bank": "I'll remind you at 3:00 PM, sir: call the bank." At three: "You asked me to remind you at 3:00 PM, sir: call the bank." The line always names the time, so it reads right in a catch-up or after a restart. The model works out the time from yours, written as your clock reads it, so the clocks changing on October 25 or November 1 doesn't move it; yapd only checks that it's still to come and at most thirty days ahead ("That time's already gone, sir." / "That's further out than I keep reminders, sir."); "in a minute" said late in a minute is kept for thirty seconds on, never taken for the past. What you say in the same breath, like "and tell the Mina one to rebase", is done now.
- "Tell me when the Tezos one finishes" ("I'll tell you when the Tezos importer finishes, sir.", or that it isn't running now and you'll hear when it next finishes) and "let me know when it asks me something". When it finishes, it's one item: "The Tezos importer has finished, sir, as you asked." then its update, whatever you muted, in focus or quiet, never skipped as slight. With no update to say, as for a quick turn, or when its update is dropped before you hear it, as when the thread starts again, the line comes on its own; a failure is said with it, followed by what you asked to be reminded of, or, when a limit was said for another thread already or the failure isn't said after all, "The Tezos importer stopped, sir, before finishing: it hit Claude's limit." Once a failure is waiting to be said with it, the reminder has gone off: cancelling it then gets "It's already gone off, sir." A run you stopped leaves it waiting, and so does one that ended before you asked: it's the next that counts. A run that ended while yapd was down or rig was out of reach counts the same way once yapd sees it: one you stopped leaves it waiting, and one that failed gets the "stopped, before finishing" line with why. A turn its hook tells of goes by how T3 Code says its run ended: one that failed leads its update with "The Tezos importer stopped, sir, before finishing: …" and why, one you stopped leaves it waiting, and when yapd can't read how it went, as when that machine's T3 Code is out of reach, it's "The Tezos importer has stopped working on it, sir, as you asked to hear.", never that it finished. Asked when it's already asking you something: "The Tezos importer is asking you something now, sir.", and nothing is kept. yapd doesn't watch pull requests: asked to, it says so.
- "At five, offer to tell the std fix to ship it", or "tell the std fix to ship it at five": "At 5:00 PM I'll offer to tell the std fix: ship it, sir." (a message that can't be read aloud is "what you said"). At five, once nothing else is being asked and you're not dictating: "Shall I tell the std fix: ship it, sir?" Only a yes sends it, under ids of its own, checked like any message for the same words sent just before; a no sends nothing; unanswered it's asked once more, then let go. An offer can wait on a thread finishing or asking too. One whose thread is gone is said in words: "…It's gone from T3 Code."
- "What reminders do I have?" is answered from what's kept. "Cancel the bank reminder" gives "Cancelled your reminder at 3:00 PM, sir: call the bank.", and "scratch that" right after brings it back ("Kept your reminder…"). When the model isn't sure which you meant, like two that both say to call the bank, nothing is cancelled: "Which reminder, sir: call the bank at 3:00 PM or call the bank at 5:00 PM?", and "the second one", or naming it, cancels that one, whatever was set or went off meanwhile. Archiving or deleting a thread drops what you set on it, said once.
- The menu bar app's Reminders menu: what's still to come, each with its time or its thread as yapd says it, like "Call the bank" with "At 3:00 PM" under it, or "When the Tezos migration finishes", even while yapd is off, and "No Reminders" when there are none. Hover over one and choose Cancel: it's cancelled as "cancel the bank reminder" does, with nothing said, and "scratch that" by voice within two minutes keeps it again ("Kept your reminder…"). One that goes off or is cancelled while the menu is open turns grey where it is, Cancel too, so nothing moves under the pointer. One refused anyway, as when it went off just before, gets yapd's reason under Reminders the next time the menu opens, like "It's already gone off.", and it's gone from the list then. Hover over one for all of it, however long. Cancelled from the menu just as you cancel it by voice, "cancel the bank reminder" gets "It's already cancelled, sir." Setting stays voice-only. `reminders` in the state and `DELETE /reminders/{id}` do the same for any UI, with no database step.
- Setting, offering and cancelling need your own words: nothing faint, or over yapd's own voice, is kept. Your words are kept as you said them beside what the model made of them.

Never for "Mute.", nor for dictation, which has its own sound. It's never played over a line, and never twice for one reply.
- "Scratch that" by the shortcut right after focus, quiet, "I'm back", "talk shorter" or "give me everything", said by the shortcut or over an update, puts back what was on before, said plainly ("Back to normal, sir.", "Only what needs you until 4:10, sir." or "I'll keep it short, sir."), and so does a no to its line, "No, scratch that." too, rather than a message sent a minute before being withdrawn in its place; "scratch that" again puts it back as you'd set it. That's for two minutes, never once the menu or its time running out has set it since, even back just as it was ("There's nothing to take back, sir."). Said over an update, "scratch that" goes to the model reading it, which is told to ask you to say it by the shortcut, never to pass it to the agent. A mode or a mute change is kept in memory only, so after a restart "scratch that" can reach what came before it, like a message sent a minute earlier.
- "Start a thread for …" said faintly back to something, too short to be yours, is never acted on, however it's worked out; dictated, it's yours as ever.
- "Mute." on its own after more than two and a half seconds of other talk, like a TV's cut off mid-sentence with "and…", turns yapd off wherever a reply is being worked out; after a short lead-in like "Can you…", "mute." is still the rest of what you were saying.
- Never answered, beyond "Mute.": a thread waiting on a secret or on what yapd can't put in words, "your reminders went with it", a usage warning, "Looking, sir…", anything else said again from the menu, like what a thread waits on you for or an offer you set, a late line that reads your message back ("Sent the queued message: …"), and a line that says a message of yours may not have gone, or speaks of sending it again, without saying its words, since "send it again" could only reach the agent as those words: a late "couldn't send it", a message, or an answer in your own words, that didn't go with nothing offered, an offer to send one again, or "I sent that a minute ago. Again?", that yapd didn't get to ask, or let go unanswered, and what a restart couldn't confirm. Off still never listens.
- The log says, for each line, how long the microphone stayed open after it against how long it would have before, and what waited behind it ("Window: …"), and "Ticked", "Onset right after the tick" and "Speech right after the tick" (within 0.8 seconds of it, long enough for Bluetooth's lag), so the tick can be checked on your speakers. A reply to a line saying what yapd did that's too faint to be taken is logged as "Not taking … as a follow-up after what it did", so the live check counts how often that's let go. No database step: whether you're at hand is kept in memory only.

## Faster

- "Yes" or "go ahead" to an update that asked something, and "thanks" or "skip it", are handled without the model: about two seconds saved on the most common replies. A yes goes that way only when you begin it within three seconds of the update's end and say it clearly (not on `dev` yet): later or fainter, it may be the room's, so the model works it out.
- Short lines like "Right away, sir." are rendered once and kept; your usual lines are written in your `YAPD_STYLE` once and rendered at startup.
- Your own lines for "on it", in `YAPD_ON_IT` separated by `|`, take turns in place of the written one when a reply is passed on, a message or an answer in your own words goes by voice or new work starts, never the same twice in a row.
- No more ~2 s wait for a fresh Codex thread after a quiet spell (one update in five); three are kept ready.
- The speaker gets ready while an update renders: about a second saved on nearly every update.
- You can interrupt from the first word of an update, an answer or one of its questions: it turns down at your first sound and stops once you're clearly talking, which over its first three seconds takes about a fifth of a second, and the model decides from what you said, told it may begin with yapd's own words; if only its own voice stopped it, it picks up again a word or two back, without a word. When yapd is cut off, the model is also given the rest of the line it hadn't said yet and decides from that: a bare "okay" or "thanks" said before the agent's question carries on, so you hear it, and doesn't count as talk not meant for yapd; said once you've heard the question, it's taken as before, "okay" as a yes and "thanks" as enough. In real calls an okay before the question carried on 2 of 2 times; after it, "Okay." sent a yes 2 of 2 times and "Thanks." ended it 2 of 2 times with the final wording, where an earlier one sent it as a yes; an okay over the question's last word sent a yes, as did "Sounds good."; and "Okay, thanks." to a failure ended it.
- "Who needs me?" and usage need no model call; a status question takes one, against a minute or more in the reverted attempt.

## Under the hood

- A versioned SQLite store in `~/.yapd/yapd.sqlite`, with a journal of everything heard, said and done. It survives restarts and feeds catching up.
- A live link to T3 Code's threads that reconnects and catches up on its own, and actions on threads (message, steer, stop, approve, answer, archive, rename, snooze, search, usage). Messages, stops and starts by voice go through them now.
- Every message, stop and start is written down before it goes out, under ids that make T3 Code do it once however often it's sent. A restart only looks; it never sends. Turned off and on, nothing more goes out for what you said before.
- `scripts/m2-probe.ts` checks those T3 Code receipts once, on a thread it starts for itself; it sends nothing without `--send`, and `--thread` repeats only the restart check, on a thread it started.
- Groundwork, not switched on yet: streaming plus first-sentence playback for faster speech.
- `scripts/brain-eval.ts` replays real phrases against the live model to check it picks the right thread.
- yapd's local API turns away anything a web page sends, so no site you visit can have it start work or talk to your threads.
- The audio helper no longer crashes when macOS wakes the speaker and microphone without them actually playing, as it did every day or two: it checks the engine plays before each line, and plays that line the plain way instead, with the microphone told off until the next one. Rebuilding it asked once more for microphone access.
